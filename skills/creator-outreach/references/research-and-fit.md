# Research and fit

## Evidence ledger

In campaign mode, keep one ledger in the dossier. In quick mode, attach a concise source link or supplied-document attribution to each fit reason; do not output a separate ledger or scores unless asked. Keep the same evidence standard in both modes. For a campaign ledger, assign IDs such as `C1` (creator) and `B1` (brand) and record:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Claim | The specific fact supported |
| Source | Exact URL or supplied document/statement |
| Provenance | `inspected-public`, `supplied-document`, or `user-reported` |
| Source date | Date on the source or metric reporting period, if known |
| Checked on | Actual inspection date; leave unset if not inspected |
| Limits | Inaccessible profile, older campaign, unverified analytics, etc. |

Read the underlying source where possible; a search-result snippet is a lead, not a checked claim. Prefer official brand/product, campaign, partnership and contact pages. Use independent reporting for corroboration or discovery; do not turn directory entries into proof of current employment. Distinguish a retrieved fact, the user's claim and your proposed idea.

Research only information relevant to professional fit. Use public business contact routes. Do not infer personal email patterns or demographic traits from people's identities. Do not access private accounts or bypass access controls.

## Bounded discovery

1. Build search terms from content format, category, market and collaboration goal.
2. Find a modest candidate pool, normally no more than twice the requested shortlist size.
3. Inspect an official page for the brand's relevant product, activity or positioning.
4. Check exclusions and feasibility before further contact research.
5. Inspect an official partnership/contact route if available. Record `contact not found` rather than guessing.
6. Stop at a useful shortlist or report the shortage. A weak match is not improved by a confident score.

Do not equate a social hashtag, follower count or historic creator post with an open campaign. Record campaign dates. Use `current` only when an inspected source supports activity for the relevant time window. If timing cannot be established, describe the specific precedent without a freshness claim.

## Choose the outreach route

Choose the route that owns the relevant brief. Record its source and whether it is current, historical or supplied-only.

| Route | When it fits | What to prepare |
|---|---|---|
| Direct brand | An official brand page names a partnerships team or accepts relevant proposals | A product-specific concept and one appropriate business contact |
| Campaign agency | A current official campaign/brand brief identifies an agency handling creator work | Address the named campaign, match its deliverables and ask about that brief's submission process |
| Influencer platform | An official brand link or supplied brief points to a campaign application | Match the application fields, market, eligibility and deadline; use the stated route |

An agency's old case study does not establish that it still represents the brand. A platform listing does not prove the campaign is open. Check the underlying page and date; if access or eligibility is unclear, label it rather than claiming an application was completed. Preserve the brand as the thread identity and coordinate one approach across brand/agency/platform routes. Put route details in `contact_route` and `notes`; extra tracker columns are optional.

## Find a supported reason now

Look for a dated launch, an open brief, a store opening, an exhibition, a local event or a creator's supplied upcoming project. A seasonal concept can be relevant without proving that a brand is commissioning it. Preserve source dates and distinguish a proposed seasonal angle from a confirmed campaign.

Planning cycles vary by brand, category and market. Do not assert that every brand plans Q4 in summer or has budget because a season is approaching. When planning timing matters, ask the relevant team when upcoming briefs are reviewed. If no hook is evidenced, lead with the creative fit and omit urgency.

## Eligibility before ranking

Mark each candidate `eligible`, `excluded`, or `needs clarification`. Exclude a known conflict rather than subtracting a few scoring points. If an unresolved condition could disqualify the match, keep it out of a ready-to-contact list until clarified. Distinguish a promising research lead from a qualified outreach candidate.

## Transparent fit rubric

For each dimension use `0 = evidenced mismatch`, `1 = plausible partial fit`, `2 = specific supported fit`, or `? = insufficient information`.

| Dimension | Evidence to seek |
|---|---|
| Audience/context | Shared interests or context supported by supplied analytics or actual content; do not assume geography/demographics |
| Creative fit | A specific product, programme or brand activity the creator can work with credibly |
| Format feasibility | A deliverable compatible with demonstrated work and the requested partnership type |
| Geography | Brand sells, ships or operates where the creator can deliver; travel is feasible if needed |

Display all four dimensions and evidence coverage, e.g. `2 / 2 / 2 / ?; 3 of 4 assessed`. Sum to a total out of eight only when all four are assessed.

Timing is not scored, because it is rarely knowable from public sources. Record it separately as `timing: [evidenced hook / unknown / conflict]`. An evidenced hook (a launch, a season, a dated campaign) strengthens the pitch's "why now". A known conflict (such as dates the creator can't do) belongs under exclusions. Do not silently turn unknowns into zero or compare incomplete totals with complete totals. Prefer better-supported candidates; explain ordering that depends on judgment. No score establishes affordability, reply likelihood or intent to commission.

For a roster, show the recommended creator for each brand and the differentiating fit. Avoid sending competing pitches from several roster members. Record the chosen thread owner.
