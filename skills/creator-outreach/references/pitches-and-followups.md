# Pitches and follow-ups

## First email

Build a short email from these elements, adapting the order to the strongest evidence:

1. Introduce the real sender and creator in one sentence.
2. State a supported brand-specific connection.
3. Propose concrete content, art or a performance and its intended use.
4. Offer one relevant proof point if supplied; otherwise link to actual work.
5. Close with one easy next step, such as asking whether this belongs with the partnerships team.

Use exact supplied creator links and a consistent manager/creator perspective. Avoid invented product use, brand marketing needs, guaranteed conversions, vague "synergy", fake scarcity and unsubstantiated superlatives.

Working structure:

> Subject A: [Specific idea] with [Creator]
> Subject B: [Creator] × [Brand]: [format]
>
> Hi [confirmed name or Brand team],
>
> I'm [Sender], [Creator]'s manager / [creator introduction]. [One true sentence about the creator's work.]
>
> [Supported brand detail] made me think of [proposed concept]. We could explore [bounded deliverable] built around [creative connection].
>
> [Optional supported proof point.] Instagram: [exact link]. TikTok: [exact link].
>
> Would you be open to [one next step]?
>
> [Sender]

Replace all placeholders before labelling a draft ready. Do not invent a named recipient to complete the template.

## Short DM

Default to 40–70 words. Introduce the creator, give one specific idea and ask for the appropriate business contact. Do not paste a full email, repeatedly DM several staff, or imply a relationship that does not exist.

## Follow-ups

Use actual thread context. A first follow-up can add a clearer concept or a relevant sample; a final follow-up can close the loop politely. Do not invent new results or deadlines as a reason to follow up.

Suggest, rather than schedule, two follow-ups at most unless the user specifies another appropriate sequence. Read current tracking and replies each time. Any reply pauses the cold sequence. An out-of-office message needs a return-date decision, not an automatic follow-up.

## Commercial replies

Identify interest, information requests, gifted/affiliate offers, negotiation, declines and redirections. Preserve the original paid/gifted/affiliate goal.

Before proposing a fee or agreeing a scope, separate:

- Deliverables, production effort and revisions.
- Posting channels and delivery dates.
- Organic use versus paid advertising, duration and territory.
- Exclusivity, account permissions and supplied conflicts.
- Travel, costs, payment timing and supplied minimums.

These are questions to resolve, not default entitlements. Do not present a generic rate as this creator's price. If the user requests options without a rate card, provide a labelled hypothetical structure and ask for their minimum before sending a commitment.

Keep private replies and commercial terms out of public examples. Do not make legal conclusions about contract enforceability; help identify terms for review.
